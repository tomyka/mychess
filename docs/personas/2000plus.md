He will compare mychess directly to Chessbook and ChessReps and will notice performance problems, wrong notation, and lost annotations immediately.

## Goals

- Keep deep preparation (20+ moves, many branches) fresh between tournaments without re-reading files.
- Be reminded of the exact move order in rare sidelines he has not seen over the board for months.
- Prepare for a specific opponent before a round: check their games against his repertoire.
- Reach FM (2300) within two seasons.

## Frustrations

- Tools that choke on large repertoires (thousands of positions) or render slowly.
- PGN import that mangles NAGs ($1, $14), long comments, or nested variations.
- Being forced into a learn pass for 500 positions he already knows; he wants "mark as known" or a quick placement test.
- Opponent-move branches cluttering review when only his own moves matter.
- Being treated as a beginner by default (starter repertoires, hints, arrows).

## Training habits

- 30-45 minutes a day of review, longer before tournaments.
- Imports and updates his own repertoires from PGN after each preparation session.
- Uses the explorer panel heavily, including master-game stats.
- Forks coach material and extends it; shares the fork back so the coach can follow.
- Runs his own small coaching side-line: 2-3 beginners linked to him as coach.

## What success means

- A 2,000+ position repertoire imports in seconds and drills without lag on the iPad.
- Review focuses on his moves; opponent alternatives are context, not cards.
- After a tournament, imported games show exactly where theory ended and who deviated.
- He can be a player (with Rimantas as coach) and a coach (for his students) in one account without confusion.
