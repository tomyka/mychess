Gabija played junior national championships and plays in the Lithuanian team league for her club. She has outgrown "just know the first moves" and now loses games because she half-knows main lines. She uses Chess.com and Lichess for blitz and keeps her own notes in a messy PGN file.

## Goals

- Main-line understanding of her Sicilian (Taimanov) and her Queen's Gambit to around move 12, with the key branches.
- Reach FIDE 1850 within a year and qualify for the national junior final.
- Turn her messy PGN notes into something she can actually drill.
- Get specific feedback from Rimantas on lines she keeps losing.

## Frustrations

- Tools that drill every move equally: she wants her weak branches to come up more often.
- Repertoire tools that do not handle transpositions (5.Nc3 Qc7 arriving by different move orders).
- Losing work when a PGN import silently drops variations or comments.
- Notifications that spam; she wants one daily digest.
- Having to tell her coach what she has been working on.

## Training habits

- 20-30 minutes a day of review on the phone; one longer session on the laptop on Sundays to import games and edit lines.
- Imports PGN from Chess.com study exports and Lichess studies.
- Writes personal notes on critical moves ("jei 9.Na4, tai Be7").
- Uses the explorer panel to check what is popular at her level.
- Tends to let reviews pile up during exam weeks, then clears a large backlog.

## What success means

- A due backlog of 80+ cards is still workable: she sees what is most urgent and can split it across days.
- Her weak lines are visible to her and to Rimantas without either of them compiling it.
- Her Lichess and Chess.com games show exactly where she left the repertoire.
- PGN import round-trips: what she imports, she can export again with variations and comments intact.
